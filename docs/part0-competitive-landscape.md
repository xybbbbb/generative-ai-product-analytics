# Part 0 — Competitive Landscape

## Generative AI Product Analytics

**Research Subjects:** ChatGPT, Gemini, Doubao, DeepSeek  
**Research Status:** Preliminary Competitive Analysis  
**Last Updated:** August 2026

---

## 0.1 Research Background

Generative AI products are rapidly evolving from conversational assistants into broader platforms that integrate information retrieval, reasoning, content generation, coding, data analysis, multimodal interaction, and agentic workflows.

As competition intensifies, the competitive landscape of generative AI is no longer determined solely by model performance. Product experience, ecosystem integration, use cases, pricing, personalization, and the ability to convert users into long-term and paying customers are increasingly important.

This project therefore focuses on four representative generative AI products:

- ChatGPT
- Gemini
- Doubao
- DeepSeek

Rather than attempting to determine which product is objectively "the best," this study examines how different products create user value and how those differences may influence user behavior, engagement, retention, and willingness to pay.

This competitive analysis serves as the foundation for the subsequent user research and quantitative analysis.

---

# 0.2 Research Objectives

The objectives of this section are to:

1. Understand the positioning and strategic direction of the four AI products.
2. Compare their major product capabilities and user scenarios.
3. Analyze differences in ecosystem and monetization strategies.
4. Identify potential sources of competitive differentiation.
5. Develop research hypotheses that can later be tested using first-hand user data.

The key principle of this section is:

> **Competitive analysis generates hypotheses; user research tests them.**

Therefore, statements about user preferences or behavioral differences in this section should be treated as hypotheses rather than final conclusions.

# 0.4 Product Analysis — ChatGPT

## 0.4.1 Product Positioning

ChatGPT began primarily as a conversational generative AI assistant but has increasingly developed into a broader productivity and work platform.

OpenAI describes ChatGPT as an AI assistant that can support everyday activities such as brainstorming, writing, studying, planning, mathematics, coding, and analyzing images and files. It also supports web search and other advanced tools.

Its product direction can therefore be summarized as:

> **AI Assistant → Productivity Platform**

The product is increasingly designed not only to answer questions but also to help users complete multi-step tasks.

---

## 0.4.2 Major Capabilities

Major ChatGPT capabilities include:

- Conversational AI
- Advanced reasoning
- Web search
- Deep research
- File analysis
- Image generation
- Voice interaction
- Coding assistance
- Data analysis
- Custom GPTs
- Agentic workflows
- Business and enterprise collaboration

The combination of these capabilities allows ChatGPT to participate in multiple stages of a user's workflow rather than functioning solely as a question-answering tool.

---

## 0.4.3 Monetization

ChatGPT follows a typical freemium-to-premium model.

As of 2026, OpenAI provides several consumer subscription levels, including:

- Free
- Go
- Plus
- Pro

OpenAI's current US pricing lists ChatGPT Go at $8/month, Plus at $20/month, and Pro at $200/month. The exact features and limits vary by plan and may change over time.

ChatGPT also provides Business and Enterprise offerings for organizational users.

This creates a monetization structure that can be summarized as:

> **Free access → Premium subscription → Professional / Enterprise use**

This structure makes ChatGPT a particularly useful case for studying willingness to pay.

---

## 0.4.4 Ecosystem Strategy

ChatGPT's ecosystem strategy is primarily AI-native.

Instead of relying on a pre-existing consumer ecosystem such as a search engine, email service, or mobile operating system, ChatGPT increasingly attempts to become the user's central AI interface for:

- Information
- Research
- Writing
- Coding
- Data analysis
- Content creation
- Task execution

This creates an important strategic question:

> Can an AI-native platform become a primary productivity interface?

---

## 0.4.5 Research Hypothesis

### H1 — Cross-Scenario Usage Hypothesis

ChatGPT users may demonstrate relatively high cross-scenario usage.

For example, a single user may use ChatGPT for:

- Studying
- Writing
- Coding
- Research
- Data analysis
- Daily problem solving

This hypothesis will be tested in later user research.

# 0.5 Product Analysis — Gemini

## 0.5.1 Product Positioning

Gemini represents a different product strategy from ChatGPT.

Its major competitive advantage is closely connected to Google's existing ecosystem.

Gemini is integrated with or connected to a range of Google products and services, including:

- Google Search
- Gmail
- Google Docs
- Google Drive
- Google Photos
- NotebookLM
- Google AI Studio
- Google Flow
- Other Google AI services

Its strategic direction can therefore be summarized as:

> **AI Assistant → Google Ecosystem**

Rather than requiring users to adopt a completely new workflow, Gemini can place AI capabilities inside services that users already use.

---

## 0.5.2 Major Capabilities

Gemini currently supports or provides access to:

- General conversational assistance
- Advanced reasoning
- Deep Research
- Image generation and editing
- Multimodal interaction
- File analysis
- Coding
- Google Search integration
- Google Workspace integration
- NotebookLM-related workflows
- Creative generation
- Agentic capabilities

The exact availability of features varies according to region, account type, product version, and subscription tier.

---

## 0.5.3 Monetization

Google has developed a multi-level AI subscription structure.

Current consumer plans include:

- Free
- Google AI Plus
- Google AI Pro
- Google AI Ultra

Paid plans increase access to models and AI features and can also bundle other Google services.

For example, Google AI Pro provides expanded access to Gemini capabilities, Deep Research, Google Search AI features, Gemini in Google applications, and other AI-related services.

This means Gemini's monetization strategy is not simply:

> **Pay for a better chatbot**

but increasingly:

> **Pay for a broader Google AI ecosystem.**

This distinction is highly relevant to our later analysis of willingness to pay.

---

## 0.5.4 Ecosystem Strategy

Gemini's most important strategic characteristic is ecosystem integration.

A user who already relies heavily on:

- Gmail
- Google Docs
- Google Search
- Google Drive
- Android
- YouTube
- Google Photos

may have additional reasons to use Gemini.

This creates a potential network between:

> **Existing ecosystem usage → AI adoption → increased AI usage → stronger ecosystem engagement**

Whether this relationship actually exists at the user level will be tested later.

---

## 0.5.5 Research Hypothesis

### H2 — Ecosystem Dependence Hypothesis

Users with higher dependence on Google's existing ecosystem may be more likely to use Gemini frequently.

Potential variables for later research include:

- Google Search usage
- Gmail usage
- Google Docs usage
- Android usage
- Google Drive usage
- Gemini usage frequency

The purpose of the later survey is to determine whether ecosystem dependence is statistically associated with Gemini preference or usage intensity.

# 0.7 Product Analysis — DeepSeek

## 0.7.1 Product Positioning

DeepSeek represents a somewhat different development path.

Its product ecosystem includes:

- Web application
- Mobile application
- API
- Publicly documented models and research materials

DeepSeek therefore has both:

> **Consumer AI assistant**

and

> **Model / developer platform**

characteristics.

Its strategic direction can be summarized as:

> **AI Model → Consumer Assistant + Developer Platform**

---

## 0.7.2 Model and Product Development

DeepSeek has continued to release new models and capabilities.

Its official transparency center lists DeepSeek-V4 as a major 2026 model release.

The current product emphasizes advanced reasoning and increasingly capable agentic workflows.

This makes DeepSeek particularly relevant to research questions involving:

- Complex reasoning
- Programming
- Technical work
- Research
- Model preference
- Developer usage

---

## 0.7.3 Developer and API Ecosystem

Unlike a purely consumer-oriented chatbot, DeepSeek also provides API access.

This creates two potentially different user populations:

1. Individual users interacting directly with the AI assistant.
2. Developers or organizations integrating DeepSeek models into other applications.

For this project, the first group will be the primary focus, while the second group provides useful context for understanding the product's broader business model.

---

## 0.7.4 Monetization

DeepSeek currently provides free access to its consumer-facing AI experience while also operating an API platform.

This creates a monetization path different from a pure consumer subscription model:

> **Free consumer access → Developer/API monetization**

This is an important contrast with subscription-heavy models such as ChatGPT and Google AI plans.

---

## 0.7.5 Research Hypothesis

### H4 — Technical Usage Hypothesis

DeepSeek users may demonstrate a relatively high concentration of:

- Programming
- Technical research
- Complex reasoning
- Academic tasks
- High-complexity problem solving

This should be treated as a hypothesis rather than an established fact.

The survey will test whether DeepSeek users actually differ from users of the other three products in terms of use cases and task complexity.

# 0.9 Competitive Strategy Comparison

The four products can be interpreted as representing four partially different strategies.

### ChatGPT

> **Become the user's general-purpose AI work interface.**

Its competitive strategy emphasizes breadth of capabilities, deep task completion, advanced tools, and increasing integration into professional workflows.

---

### Gemini

> **Make AI a native layer across Google's ecosystem.**

Its competitive advantage may come not only from the Gemini model itself, but from its connection to Search, Gmail, Docs, Drive, Android, YouTube, and other Google services.

---

### Doubao

> **Make AI accessible across everyday consumer scenarios.**

Its strategy emphasizes broad functionality, low-friction interaction, Chinese-language scenarios, content creation, search, and daily productivity.

---

### DeepSeek

> **Combine strong models with accessible consumer products and developer access.**

Its product strategy combines direct consumer access with an API and model ecosystem, creating opportunities to serve both individual users and developers.

# 0.10 Multi-Homing as a Key Research Concept

One of the most important assumptions for the subsequent research is that users may not treat these products as mutually exclusive.

A user may simultaneously use:

- ChatGPT for writing
- Gemini for Google-related tasks
- Doubao for Chinese-language everyday use
- DeepSeek for programming or complex reasoning

Therefore, this project will not assume that AI users make a simple "winner-takes-all" choice.

Instead, we will investigate:

> **Multi-homing behavior**

where users adopt multiple AI products for different tasks.

This distinction is important because a product can have:

- High awareness
- High trial rate
- High occasional usage

without necessarily having:

- High primary usage
- High engagement
- High retention
- High willingness to pay

These variables should therefore be measured separately in later stages.

# 0.13 Limitations of the Competitive Analysis

This section is based primarily on publicly available product information and therefore has several limitations.

### 1. Product information changes rapidly

Generative AI products frequently update:

- Models
- Features
- Pricing
- Usage limits
- Product interfaces
- Regional availability

Therefore, the competitive matrix should be updated before the final version of the project is submitted or presented.

### 2. Product features do not equal user value

The existence of a feature does not necessarily mean that users:

- Know about it
- Use it
- Like it
- Consider it important
- Are willing to pay for it

Therefore, feature comparisons alone cannot establish competitive advantage.

### 3. Public information does not directly reveal user behavior

Official product pages can describe intended product capabilities, but they cannot tell us:

- Why users choose a product
- How frequently users use it
- Why users stop using it
- Which features users actually value
- Whether users are willing to pay

These questions require first-hand user research.

### 4. User segmentation remains unverified

The proposed user differences between ChatGPT, Gemini, Doubao, and DeepSeek are research hypotheses rather than conclusions.

# 0.15 Preliminary Conclusion

The preliminary competitive analysis indicates that ChatGPT, Gemini, Doubao, and DeepSeek should not be viewed simply as four interchangeable AI chatbots.

Instead, they represent different approaches to creating value in the generative AI market:

| Product | Preliminary Strategic Position |
|---|---|
| ChatGPT | AI-native productivity and general-purpose assistant |
| Gemini | AI integrated into the Google ecosystem |
| Doubao | Broad, consumer-oriented, multi-scenario AI assistant |
| DeepSeek | Model capability + consumer assistant + developer/API ecosystem |

The key implication for this research is that competitive advantage may come from different sources.

For one product, the key factor may be **workflow depth**.

For another, it may be **ecosystem integration**.

For another, it may be **scenario breadth**.

For another, it may be **technical capability**.

The subsequent user research will therefore examine whether these product-level differences translate into measurable differences in:

> **User choice → Usage → Engagement → Retention → Willingness to Pay**

rather than assuming that product features automatically generate user value.
